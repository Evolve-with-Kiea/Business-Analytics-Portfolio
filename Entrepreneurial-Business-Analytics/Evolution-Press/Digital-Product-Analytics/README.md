# Digital Product Analytics

## Shop the Evolution™ | Evolution Press® | Little Bosses™

### Digital Commerce, Product Conversion, Customer Demand & Revenue Performance

## Project Overview

**Digital Product Analytics** is a planned Business Analytics portfolio project focused on evaluating the performance of downloadable products, digital educational resources, templates, workbooks, and related digital commerce offerings.

The project is designed around **Shop the Evolution™**, with potential applications to Evolution Press®, Little Bosses™, and future educational and business development products.

The primary objective is to understand how customers discover, evaluate, purchase, and access digital products—and how those activities contribute to business revenue, product profitability, and future product development decisions.

The analysis will distinguish between product interest, free resource engagement, completed paid transactions, and successful digital delivery.

This distinction is especially important because a product may generate substantial page views or downloads without generating paid revenue.

The project will initially focus on Excel-based data analysis using fictional or appropriately authorized datasets. As analytical skills and business data availability develop, the project may expand into SQL, Python, Power Query, and Power BI.

# 2. Business Problem

A digital product business may attract website visitors and generate downloads without developing a sustainable revenue stream.

For example:

- A free workbook may receive numerous downloads but generate no direct sales.
- A paid template may receive product page visits but have a low purchase conversion rate.
- A bundle may generate higher order value while reducing contribution through discounts.
- A digital guide may sell through multiple channels with different transaction fees.
- Customers may purchase a product but experience delivery or access difficulties.
- Promotional campaigns may generate sales that do not justify acquisition spending.

Without structured analytics, it may be difficult to identify which products deserve further development, promotion, or pricing adjustments.

### Central Business Question

**How can Shop the Evolution™ use digital product data to evaluate customer demand, product conversion, paid sales, download activity, and financial performance to support sustainable digital commerce growth?**

# 7. Digital Product Sales Definitions

Consistent definitions are essential for accurate reporting.

| Event | Definition | Counts as Paid Sale? |
|---|---|---|
| Product Page View | Recorded visit or view of a product page | No |
| Add to Cart | Product added to shopping cart | No |
| Checkout Started | Checkout process initiated | No |
| Order Created | Order record generated | Not necessarily |
| Paid Order | Order with confirmed successful payment | Yes, under a paid-order definition |
| Refunded Order | Previously paid order refunded in full or part | Requires separate treatment |
| Free Download | Digital file accessed without payment | No |
| Download Attempt | Customer initiates a file download | No |
| Successful Download | Defined successful delivery or download event | No additional sale |
| Repeat Download | Previously acquired file downloaded again | No additional sale |

### Measurement Standard

**One paid order does not necessarily equal one unit sold, one unique customer, or one download.**

For example, a single order may contain multiple digital products, and a customer may download the same purchased file more than once.

# 8. Digital Download Analytics

Download data can help evaluate product delivery and access, but it must be interpreted carefully.

### Potential Metrics

- Unique purchasers
- Eligible digital orders
- Download links issued
- Successful downloads
- Download attempts
- Repeat downloads
- Delivery failures
- Support requests

### Potential KPIs

**Download Activation Rate**

Purchasers with at least one verified successful download ÷ eligible purchasers × 100

**Delivery Failure Rate**

Defined failed delivery events ÷ eligible delivery events × 100

### Important Limitations

Not all digital delivery platforms provide the same tracking capabilities.

A missing download event does not automatically mean the customer failed to receive the product.

Download counts also do not establish whether the customer opened, read, completed, or benefited from the resource.

# 9. Product Page Conversion Analysis

## Purpose

Determine how effectively product pages encourage eligible visitors to complete purchases.

### Simplified Formula

**Product Page Purchase Conversion Rate = Purchases attributed to the product page ÷ eligible product page visits × 100**

The numerator, denominator, attribution approach, and reporting period must be documented.

### Illustrative Example

A fictional digital workbook receives 500 eligible product page visits and generates 20 attributed purchases.

**Conversion Rate = 20 ÷ 500 × 100 = 4%**

### Potential Analytical Questions

- Does the page clearly communicate product benefits?
- Is pricing visible and understandable?
- Are product previews available?
- Is the checkout process functioning?
- Are visitors arriving from relevant traffic sources?
- Does mobile performance differ from desktop performance?

### Important Consideration

Product page views and purchases may come from different tracking systems.

Calculations should not assume exact attribution when the data cannot establish it.

# 11. Digital Product Unit Economics

Digital products may have low marginal distribution costs, but they are not necessarily cost-free to produce or sell.

### Potential Cost Components

- Product creation and development
- Design software
- Digital delivery platform fees
- Payment processing
- Marketplace commissions
- Customer support
- Promotional discounts
- Marketing acquisition costs
- Ongoing updates and maintenance

### Illustrative Product Economics

Assume a fictional digital workbook sells for $20.

| Financial Measure | Illustrative Amount |
|---|---:|
| Selling Price | $20.00 |
| Discount | $2.00 |
| Net Sale Before Fees | $18.00 |
| Payment and Delivery Fees | $1.50 |
| Contribution Before Marketing and Fixed Costs | $16.50 |

The $16.50 is not necessarily net profit.

Product development, marketing, fixed operating expenses, taxes, and other costs may affect final profitability.

### Important Consideration

Development costs may be treated differently for accounting purposes than for management profitability analysis.

Any allocation of development costs across units sold must be documented.

# 12. Free Versus Paid Product Performance

Free resources and paid products serve different business purposes.

## Free Resource Objectives

- Introduce the brand
- Provide educational value
- Encourage relevant website engagement
- Generate permission-based newsletter subscriptions
- Introduce related products

## Paid Product Objectives

- Generate revenue
- Recover relevant costs
- Produce sustainable contribution
- Support repeat purchasing
- Validate customer willingness to pay

### Comparison Framework

| Metric | Free Resource | Paid Product |
|---|---|---|
| Page Views | Applicable | Applicable |
| Downloads | Applicable | Applicable |
| Paid Transactions | Not applicable | Applicable |
| Direct Sales Revenue | $0 | Potentially positive |
| Subscriber Conversion | If consent is collected | If consent is collected |
| Product Contribution | May be negative due to costs | Depends on revenue and costs |

### Important Privacy Principle

Downloading a free resource does not automatically constitute consent to receive marketing emails.

Newsletter consent must be obtained and recorded appropriately.

| Table | Key Fields | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Products | Product ID, category, type, price | Product catalog |
| Product Status | Product ID, status, effective date | Availability history |
| Product Views | Product ID, session ID, date | Product discovery |
| Orders | Order ID, customer ID, date, payment status | Order-level activity |
| Order Items | Order ID, product ID, quantity, amount | Product-level sales |
| Refunds | Refund ID, order item reference, amount | Sales adjustments |
| Downloads | Download event ID, product ID, order reference | Digital access |
| Customers | Customer ID, acquisition date | Customer-level analysis |
| Bundles | Bundle ID, component product IDs | Bundle relationships |
| Marketing | Campaign ID, source, spending | Marketing analysis |
| Product Costs | Product ID, cost category, amount | Unit economics |

### Data Modeling Standards

- Use unique identifiers.
- Avoid double-counting orders and order items.
- Distinguish customers from sessions.
- Define successful download events.
- Separate free access from paid purchases.
- Record refunds consistently.
- Document channel-specific definitions.
- Protect customer information.
