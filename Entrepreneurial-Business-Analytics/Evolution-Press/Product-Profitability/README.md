# Product Profitability

## Shop the Evolution™ | Evolution Press® | Little Bosses™

### Product Pricing, Cost Analysis, Contribution Margins & Break-Even Planning

## Project Overview

**Product Profitability** is a planned Business Analytics portfolio project focused on evaluating the financial performance of physical products, digital resources, published books, and educational product bundles.

The project is designed around Shop the Evolution™, with additional applications to Evolution Press® and Little Bosses™.

Its purpose is to determine how product pricing, discounts, production expenses, platform fees, fulfillment costs, and sales volume influence financial performance.

The project will combine accounting principles, financial modeling, product analytics, and business intelligence to support more informed product decisions.

A central emphasis is distinguishing **sales revenue, gross profit, contribution margin, operating profit, and cash flow** rather than treating them as interchangeable measures.

The initial project will use fictional or documented planning assumptions in Microsoft Excel. Actual business data may be incorporated when available and appropriate.

# 2. Business Problem

A business can generate product sales without earning sufficient profit to sustain operations.

For example:

- A printed planner may sell for a premium price but have substantial production and fulfillment costs.
- A digital workbook may have low delivery costs but require meaningful development and marketing investment.
- A book sold through a marketplace may generate royalties rather than the full retail selling price.
- A discounted bundle may increase order value while reducing contribution margin.
- A product may have a positive unit contribution but fail to cover fixed operating costs.

Without structured profitability analysis, businesses may prioritize products based on sales volume or revenue rather than economic performance.

### Central Business Question

**How can Shop the Evolution™ evaluate product pricing, costs, discounts, sales channels, and break-even requirements to identify financially sustainable products and support better merchandising decisions?**

# 5. Financial Measurement Definitions

| Financial Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| List Price | Standard advertised product price before discounts |
| Actual Selling Price | Price charged after applicable product discounts |
| Gross Sales | Product sales before defined discounts and returns |
| Net Sales | Gross sales less applicable discounts, returns, and allowances |
| Cost of Goods Sold | Costs classified as product costs under the chosen accounting policy |
| Gross Profit | Net sales less cost of goods sold |
| Variable Costs | Costs that vary with the relevant activity level |
| Contribution Margin | Revenue less defined variable costs |
| Fixed Costs | Costs that do not change with activity within the relevant range |
| Operating Profit | Revenue less operating expenses under the selected accounting basis |
| Cash Flow | Actual cash received and paid during a period |

### Financial Integrity Principle

**Revenue is not profit, and a positive contribution margin does not guarantee positive operating profit.**

# 9. Unit Contribution Analysis

## Purpose

Determine how much each unit contributes toward fixed costs and potential profit after relevant variable costs.

### Formula

**Unit Contribution = Net Selling Price − Variable Cost per Unit**

### Illustrative Example

A fictional physical planner has the following economics:

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| List Price | $35.00 |
| Discount | $5.00 |
| Net Selling Price | $30.00 |
| Printing Cost | $10.00 |
| Packaging | $2.00 |
| Payment and Platform Fees | $1.50 |
| Shipping Subsidy | $3.50 |
| Total Variable Cost | $17.00 |
| Unit Contribution | $13.00 |

**Unit Contribution = $30 − $17 = $13**

### Contribution Margin Percentage

**Contribution Margin % = Unit Contribution ÷ Net Selling Price × 100**

**$13 ÷ $30 × 100 = 43.33%**

### Interpretation

Each unit generates $13 toward fixed costs and potential operating profit under the stated assumptions.

The $13 is not necessarily net profit.

# 10. Product Break-Even Analysis

## Purpose

Estimate the number of units required to recover defined fixed costs.

### Formula

**Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ Unit Contribution**

Round up to the next whole unit.

### Illustrative Example

Assume:

- Fixed costs allocated to the product scenario: $650
- Unit contribution: $13

**Break-Even Units = $650 ÷ $13 = 50 units**

### Interpretation

The business must sell 50 units under the stated assumptions to cover the $650 of defined fixed costs.

### Important Limitations

This simplified model assumes:

- Constant selling price
- Constant unit variable cost
- Constant fixed costs within the relevant range
- No significant returns or product mix changes
- All modeled units are sold and recognized consistently

The model should be adjusted when those assumptions are inappropriate.

# 11. Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

Pricing changes may have a significant effect on unit contribution.

### Illustrative Scenario

Assume unit variable costs remain $17.

| Selling Price | Unit Variable Cost | Unit Contribution | Contribution Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $25 | $17 | $8 | 32.0% |
| $30 | $17 | $13 | 43.3% |
| $35 | $17 | $18 | 51.4% |

### Analytical Questions

- How does a discount affect contribution?
- How many additional units would be needed to offset a lower price?
- What happens if production costs increase?
- What price is needed to achieve a target contribution margin?

### Important Consideration

The model should not assume that sales volume remains unchanged after a price increase or decrease without explicitly identifying that assumption.

# 12. Discount & Promotion Profitability

Promotional pricing can increase demand while reducing contribution per unit.

### Potential Promotional Types

- Percentage discounts
- Fixed-dollar discounts
- Bundle discounts
- Limited-time offers
- Free shipping promotions
- Volume discounts

### Illustrative Analysis

| Measure | Regular Price | Promotional Price |
|---|---:|---:|
| Selling Price | $30 | $25 |
| Variable Cost | $17 | $17 |
| Unit Contribution | $13 | $8 |

The promotional price reduces unit contribution by $5.

### Business Question

**How many additional units must be sold to generate the same total contribution?**

For example, 100 regular-price units generate $1,300 in contribution.

To generate at least $1,300 at $8 contribution per unit, the business would need 163 promotional units.

This represents an increase of 63 units.

The example does not establish that the promotion would generate the required additional demand.

# 20. Marketing Costs & Product Profitability

Product profitability may be evaluated before and after attributable marketing costs.

### Contribution Before Marketing

Net sales less defined product-level variable costs.

### Contribution After Marketing

Contribution before marketing less attributable marketing costs.

### Important Distinction

A product may have a positive unit contribution before marketing but become economically unattractive after customer acquisition costs.

### Related Analytical Projects

- Marketing Campaign Analytics
- Marketing ROI
- Digital Product Analytics
