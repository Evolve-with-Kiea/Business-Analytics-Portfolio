# Sales Performance

## Shop the Evolution™ | Evolution Press® | Little Bosses™

### Sales Analysis, Revenue Trends, Product Performance & Business Forecasting

## Project Overview

**Sales Performance** is a planned Business Analytics portfolio project focused on analyzing sales transactions, product performance, revenue trends, distribution channels, and future sales scenarios.

The project is designed around Shop the Evolution™, with potential applications to Evolution Press®, Little Bosses™, and other product-based initiatives within the Evolve with Kiea business ecosystem.

Its purpose is to understand what is selling, when purchases occur, which products and categories contribute to revenue, and how sales patterns may inform inventory, marketing, pricing, and financial decisions.

The project will emphasize accurate transaction definitions, consistent reporting periods, data validation, and the distinction between historical performance and projected outcomes.

The initial version will use a realistic fictional sales dataset in Microsoft Excel. Future versions may incorporate authorized actual business records and more advanced analysis using Power Query, SQL, Python, and Power BI.

# 2. Business Problem

A business may know its total sales revenue without understanding which products, categories, channels, or periods are responsible for that performance.

For example:

- One product may generate substantial revenue but relatively few transactions.
- Another product may sell frequently at a lower price.
- Seasonal promotions may temporarily increase order volume.
- Digital products may perform differently from physical products.
- Marketplace sales may be reported differently from direct website sales.
- Revenue growth may reflect higher prices rather than increased unit demand.

Without structured analysis, these differences may be overlooked.

### Central Business Question

**How can Shop the Evolution™ use sales transaction data to identify product demand, evaluate revenue performance, understand sales trends, and develop responsible forecasts that support better business decisions?**

# 4. Sales Measurement Definitions

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Order | A recorded customer purchase transaction |
| Paid Order | An order with confirmed successful payment |
| Unit Sold | One unit of a product sold |
| Gross Sales | Product sales before defined discounts and returns |
| Discounts | Price reductions applied to sales |
| Returns | Merchandise or transactions returned under the defined reporting policy |
| Refunds | Amounts refunded to customers |
| Net Sales | Gross sales less applicable discounts, returns, and allowances |
| Average Order Value | Defined order revenue divided by the corresponding order count |
| Sales Channel | Platform or arrangement through which a sale occurs |
| Sales Forecast | Estimate of future sales based on stated assumptions or a documented model |

### Important Distinctions

One order may contain several products.

One customer may place multiple orders.

A refund may occur in a different period from the original sale.

Gross sales, net sales, recognized revenue, and cash collected may differ.

# 7. Sales Performance by Time Period

Sales trends may be evaluated using consistent time intervals.

### Potential Reporting Periods

- Daily
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Quarterly
- Annual

### Potential Metrics

- Net sales by period
- Units sold by period
- Orders by period
- Average order value
- Period-over-period growth

### Month-Over-Month Growth

**MoM Growth = (Current Month Sales − Previous Month Sales) ÷ Previous Month Sales × 100**

### Year-Over-Year Growth

**YoY Growth = (Current Period Sales − Comparable Prior-Year Period Sales) ÷ Comparable Prior-Year Period Sales × 100**

Growth percentages should be marked not applicable when the comparison period has zero sales, rather than calculated through division by zero.

### Important Consideration

A startup may have insufficient history for meaningful year-over-year comparisons.

# 9. Average Order Value

## Purpose

Understand the average revenue generated per eligible order.

### Formula

**AOV = Defined Order Revenue ÷ Eligible Orders**

### Illustrative Example

A fictional business generates $3,000 in net product sales from 100 eligible paid orders.

**AOV = $3,000 ÷ 100 = $30**

### Potential Business Questions

- Does AOV change during promotions?
- Do bundles increase order value?
- Which channels generate larger orders?
- Are customers purchasing multiple products per transaction?

### Important Consideration

A higher AOV does not automatically mean higher profitability.

# 15. Sales Forecasting

## Purpose

Estimate future sales using historical data, assumptions, and documented methods.

### Potential Beginner Methods

- Simple average
- Moving average
- Growth-rate scenarios
- Product-level planning assumptions

### Potential Advanced Methods

- Time-series forecasting
- Seasonal forecasting
- Regression analysis
- Scenario-based forecasting

### Forecasting Requirements

Every forecast should document:

- Historical data used
- Forecast period
- Method
- Assumptions
- Limitations
- Whether results are actual or projected

### Important Distinction

**A forecast is an estimate, not a guaranteed outcome or a verified sale.**

---

# 16. Forecasting Example

Assume a fictional business has the following monthly net sales.

| Month | Actual Net Sales |
|---|---:|
| January | $1,000 |
| February | $1,200 |
| March | $1,400 |

A simple three-month average forecast for April would be:

**($1,000 + $1,200 + $1,400) ÷ 3 = $1,200**

### Interpretation

The illustrative April forecast is $1,200.

This forecast does not incorporate product launches, seasonality, marketing changes, or other potential business factors.

A simple average is a learning exercise rather than a complete forecasting system.
