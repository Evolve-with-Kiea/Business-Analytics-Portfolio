# Marketing Campaign Analytics

## Evolve with Kiea | Shop the Evolution™ | Brand Evolution Marketing Agency

### Campaign Performance, Marketing ROI & Data-Driven Business Growth

## Project Overview

**Marketing Campaign Analytics** is a planned Business Analytics portfolio project focused on measuring, evaluating, and interpreting the performance of marketing campaigns across multiple digital channels.

The project is designed around realistic startup marketing scenarios involving Shop the Evolution™, Evolve with Kiea, and future marketing services offered through Brand Evolution Marketing Agency.

The primary objective is to understand how marketing activities contribute to brand awareness, website traffic, lead generation, customer acquisition, product sales, and business growth.

Rather than evaluating marketing success solely through impressions, followers, or engagement, the analysis will connect campaign objectives with measurable results, costs, conversions, and potential financial outcomes.

The project will begin with a manageable Excel-based analysis and may later expand into SQL, Python, Power BI, and more advanced business intelligence applications.

**Project Status:** Planned — Not Yet Completed

**Business Application:** Startup marketing, e-commerce, publishing, digital products, and professional services

**Potential Tools:** Microsoft Excel, Power Query, SQL, Python, Power BI

**Data Approach:** Authorized business data, publicly available datasets with appropriate permissions, or clearly labeled synthetic campaign data

**Primary Deliverable:** A documented marketing campaign performance analysis and reporting dashboard.

# 2. Business Problem

Startup businesses often have limited marketing budgets and must decide where to invest their time, effort, and available resources.

A campaign may generate substantial impressions without producing website visits, while another campaign may generate fewer impressions but attract more qualified visitors.

Similarly, high click-through rates do not necessarily indicate profitable marketing performance.

For example:

- A Pinterest campaign may generate website traffic but limited purchases.
- An Instagram campaign may generate engagement without measurable conversions.
- A Facebook post may generate meaningful conversations or inquiries.
- An email campaign may drive repeat visits or purchases.
- A paid campaign may produce sales but cost more than the contribution generated.

Without a structured analytical approach, business owners may struggle to determine which activities deserve continued investment.

### Central Business Question

**How can marketing campaign data be used to identify which promotional activities generate meaningful business outcomes, control acquisition costs, and support better marketing decisions across a growing business ecosystem?**

# 4. Campaign Objectives & Measurement Strategy

Every campaign should begin with a clearly defined objective.

| Campaign Objective | Primary KPI | Supporting Metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Brand Awareness | Reach or impressions | Frequency, engagement |
| Website Traffic | Landing page visits | Clicks, CTR, engaged sessions |
| Lead Generation | Qualified leads | Cost per lead, lead conversion rate |
| Newsletter Growth | Confirmed subscriptions | Signup rate, cost per subscriber |
| Product Sales | Purchases or net sales | Conversion rate, AOV, attributed revenue |
| Book Promotion | Purchases or qualified product visits | Referral clicks, attributed sales |
| Client Acquisition | Qualified inquiries or clients acquired | Proposal conversion, acquisition cost |
| Content Discovery | Relevant article visits | Referral traffic, engagement |

### Measurement Principle

The campaign objective determines which metrics should receive the greatest attention.

An awareness campaign should not be judged exclusively by purchases when its stated purpose is increasing visibility.

Likewise, a sales campaign should not be declared successful solely because it generated impressions.

# 8. Marketing KPI Definitions

| KPI | Formula | Business Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Click-Through Rate | Clicks ÷ impressions × 100 | Proportion of impressions generating clicks |
| Cost Per Click | Advertising spend ÷ ad clicks | Average advertising cost per click |
| Cost Per Result | Defined campaign cost ÷ attributed results | Cost of the specified result |
| Conversion Rate | Conversions ÷ defined eligible visits or clicks × 100 | Proportion converting |
| Cost Per Lead | Defined lead acquisition cost ÷ attributed leads | Average cost per lead |
| Cost Per Acquisition | Defined acquisition cost ÷ acquired customers | Average acquisition cost |
| Return on Ad Spend | Attributed advertising revenue ÷ advertising spend | Revenue attributed per advertising dollar |
| Average Order Value | Defined order revenue ÷ orders | Average revenue per order |

### Important Calculation Standards

- Specify whether clicks represent all clicks, link clicks, or outbound clicks.
- Define what qualifies as a result.
- Use comparable reporting periods.
- Record currency and spending basis.
- Avoid division by zero.
- Document attribution assumptions.
- Do not compare incompatible platform metrics without qualification.

# 9. Campaign Budget & Spending Analysis

### Purpose

Evaluate planned marketing investment against actual spending.

### Budget Tracker

| Campaign | Planned Budget | Actual Spend | Budget Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Campaign A | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Calculated] |
| Campaign B | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Calculated] |
| Campaign C | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Calculated] |

**Budget Variance = Actual Spend − Planned Budget**

A positive variance represents spending above budget under this definition.

### Potential Spending Categories

- Advertising platform costs
- Creative production
- Contractor services
- Promotional materials
- Campaign-specific software
- Agency service fees, when applicable

### Spending Definition

The project must distinguish between:

**Advertising Spend:** Amount paid to advertising platforms.

**Campaign Cost:** Advertising spend plus other defined campaign-specific costs.

**Marketing Operating Expense:** Broader marketing-related expenses that may not be attributable to one campaign.

**Founder Time:** Time invested in planning, producing, or managing campaigns, potentially valued separately for managerial analysis.

These categories should not be used interchangeably.

# 10. Illustrative Campaign Performance Analysis

The following dataset is entirely fictional and intended to demonstrate calculations.

| Campaign | Impressions | Clicks | Spend | Conversions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Campaign A | 20,000 | 600 | $180 | 18 |
| Campaign B | 15,000 | 525 | $210 | 21 |
| Campaign C | 30,000 | 450 | $150 | 9 |

Assume all clicks are measured consistently, all conversions use the same definition, and all spend represents advertising platform expenditure.

### Calculated Results

| Campaign | CTR | CPC | Click-to-Conversion Rate | Cost Per Conversion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Campaign A | 3.0% | $0.30 | 3.0% | $10.00 |
| Campaign B | 3.5% | $0.40 | 4.0% | $10.00 |
| Campaign C | 1.5% | $0.33 | 2.0% | $16.67 |

### Illustrative Interpretation

Campaign B has the highest click-through rate and click-to-conversion rate.

Campaign A has the lowest cost per click.

Campaigns A and B have equal cost per conversion.

Campaign C has the highest impression count but the highest cost per conversion.

These observations illustrate why campaign performance should be evaluated using multiple relevant metrics rather than impressions alone.

They do not establish which campaign is most profitable because conversion value and associated business costs are not provided.

# 15. Attribution & Tracking Standards

Marketing attribution attempts to associate conversions or revenue with marketing touchpoints.

### Potential Attribution Models

- Last-touch
- Platform-reported attribution

### Important Limitation

Different marketing platforms may assign credit to the same conversion.

Adding platform-reported conversions or revenue together may double-count outcomes.

The project should distinguish platform-attributed results from independently verified business transactions.

# 16. UTM Campaign Tracking

Consistent UTM parameters may help identify traffic from promotional activities.

### Standard UTM Fields

| Parameter | Purpose | Illustrative Value |
|---|---|---|
| utm_source | Traffic origin | pinterest |
| utm_medium | Marketing medium | organic_social |
| utm_campaign | Campaign identifier | planner_launch |
| utm_content | Creative variation | pin_01 |
| utm_term | Optional keyword or audience information | [Value] |

### Example

`https://shoptheevolution.com/?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=planner_launch&utm_content=pin_01`

This is an illustrative tracking URL, not a claim that the campaign or tracking configuration is active.

### Tracking Standards

- Use consistent naming conventions.
- Avoid personally identifiable information in URLs.
- Document campaign identifiers.
- Test tracking links before publishing.
- Verify that analytics tools capture intended parameters.
- Review discrepancies between platform clicks and website sessions.
