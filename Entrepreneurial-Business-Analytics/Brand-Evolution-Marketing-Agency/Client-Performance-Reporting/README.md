# Client Performance Reporting

## Brand Evolution Marketing Agency | Client Analytics, Marketing Performance & Strategic Reporting

### Project Overview

**Client Performance Reporting** is a planned Business Analytics portfolio project focused on designing a professional, repeatable reporting framework for Brand Evolution Marketing Agency.

The purpose is to demonstrate how marketing and business performance data can be translated into meaningful client reports that connect business objectives, completed activities, key performance indicators (KPIs), analytical findings, and recommended next steps.

Rather than presenting disconnected marketing metrics, the framework emphasizes helping business owners understand what happened, why the results matter, what remains uncertain, and what actions may improve performance.

The proposed framework can be adapted for entrepreneurs, small businesses, service providers, e-commerce brands, and other organizations with different marketing and business goals.

**Project Status:** Planned — Framework Development

**Business Application:** Brand Evolution Marketing Agency

**Portfolio Categories:** Business Analytics, Marketing Analytics, Business Intelligence, Client Reporting

**Potential Tools:** Microsoft Excel, Power Query, Power BI, SQL, Python, and presentation software

**Data Requirements:** Synthetic, publicly licensed, or appropriately authorized and anonymized information

**Primary Deliverable:** A reusable client performance reporting template, supported by an illustrative case study and analytical documentation.

No actual client engagement, completed report, or verified client outcome is implied by this project description.

# 1. Business Context

Brand Evolution Marketing Agency is being developed as part of the Evolve with Kiea business ecosystem.

Its potential client base includes entrepreneurs, creators, small businesses, and growing organizations seeking practical marketing support, stronger business visibility, and better understanding of performance.

A recurring challenge for small businesses is determining whether marketing activity contributes to meaningful business outcomes.

For example, a business owner may know how many people viewed an Instagram post but may not understand:

- Whether those viewers visited the website
- Whether visitors became qualified leads
- Whether leads converted into customers
- Which marketing activities contributed to sales
- Whether campaign spending produced a positive financial return
- What should be improved during the next reporting period

Client performance reporting should connect these activities into a structured business narrative.

# 4. Client Reporting Philosophy

Every report should answer five questions:

| Reporting Question | Purpose |
|---|---|
| What were we trying to accomplish? | Establish the business objective |
| What activities were completed? | Document the work performed |
| What happened? | Present verified metrics and results |
| What does it mean? | Interpret performance in business terms |

### Reporting Principle

**A strong client report should explain business performance, not simply display numbers.**

# 10. KPI Definitions & Calculation Standards

| KPI | General Calculation | Important Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion Rate | Conversions ÷ eligible visits or users × 100 | Define denominator consistently |
| Average Order Value | Order revenue ÷ orders | Document treatment of returns and taxes |
| Lead-to-Customer Rate | Converted leads ÷ eligible leads × 100 | Define conversion window |
| Customer Acquisition Cost | Attributable acquisition cost ÷ new customers acquired | Document included costs |
| Return on Ad Spend | Attributed advertising revenue ÷ advertising spending | Revenue is not profit |
| Revenue Growth | (Current revenue − prior revenue) ÷ prior revenue × 100 | Comparable periods required |
| Gross Margin | (Revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue × 100 | Cost definitions must be consistent |

Metrics should not be calculated when required information is unavailable or the denominator is inappropriate.

# 16. Financial Performance & Marketing ROI

Marketing reporting should distinguish revenue, spending, and profit.

### Important Financial Measures

| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing Spend | Costs attributed to marketing activities |
| Attributed Revenue | Revenue assigned to marketing activity under a defined attribution approach |
| Gross Profit | Revenue less associated cost of goods sold or direct service costs |
| Return on Ad Spend | Attributed revenue divided by advertising spending |
| Marketing ROI | Return calculated using an explicitly defined benefit and cost methodology |

### Illustrative Example

A fictional campaign generates $4,000 in attributed revenue and incurs $1,000 in advertising spending.

**ROAS = $4,000 ÷ $1,000 = 4.0**

This means the campaign generated $4 in attributed revenue for each $1 of advertising spending under the chosen attribution method.

It does not mean the campaign produced $3,000 in profit.

### Analytical Considerations

- Product costs
- Agency fees
- Discounts
- Returns
- Attribution assumptions
- Reporting windows
- Customer acquisition timing

**Observed Result:** [Verified metric or pattern]

**Supporting Evidence:** [Chart, table, or calculation]

**Business Interpretation:** [What the result may indicate]

**Limitations:** [What cannot be concluded]

### Reporting Principle

Distinguish between:

- What the data shows
- What may explain the result
- What additional information is needed
- What action may be reasonable

Avoid claiming that marketing activity caused an outcome when the analysis only demonstrates correlation or association.

# 24. Client Reporting Workflow

A repeatable reporting process may follow these stages:

1. Understand the client's business.
2. Identify business objectives.
3. Establish KPI definitions.
4. Identify authorized data sources.
5. Collect and validate data.
6. Prepare analytical tables.
7. Calculate performance metrics.
8. Compare results against relevant periods or targets.
9. Identify meaningful findings.
10. Develop supported recommendations.
11. Prepare the client report.
12. Review accuracy and confidentiality.
13. Present findings.
14. Document agreed next steps.
15. Evaluate outcomes in the next reporting period.

This workflow may be adapted according to project complexity and available data.
