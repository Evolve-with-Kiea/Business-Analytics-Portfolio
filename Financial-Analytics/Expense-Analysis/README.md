# 1. Purpose

The Expense Analysis folder is designed to explore how financial data can help organizations understand spending, identify changes in cost behavior, and evaluate performance against financial plans.

Expense data may include recurring operating costs, variable production expenses, administrative spending, marketing expenditures, and other business costs.

Reviewing total expenses alone may not explain whether spending is appropriate or whether a business is operating efficiently.

Financial analysis can help investigate:

- Which expense categories account for the largest share of spending?
- Which costs remain relatively stable?
- Which costs change with activity levels?
- Are expenses increasing or decreasing?
- How does actual spending compare with budget?
- Are cost changes associated with business growth?
- Which expense categories warrant further investigation?

### Central Business Question

**How can businesses use financial data to understand cost behavior, evaluate expense trends, identify budget variances, and make more informed decisions about resource allocation?**

# 4. Understanding Business Costs

Costs may be analyzed according to their behavior, purpose, or accounting classification.

### Cost Behavior

Describes how costs respond to changes in activity.

Examples:

- Fixed costs
- Variable costs
- Mixed costs
- Step costs

### Financial Statement Classification

Describes how costs are reported.

Examples:

- Cost of goods sold
- Selling expenses
- General and administrative expenses
- Other operating expenses

### Important Distinction

**A cost's behavior is not the same as its financial statement classification.**

For example, some manufacturing overhead costs may be fixed while still contributing to inventory costs under applicable accounting rules.

Each project should document the selected classification method.

# 5. Fixed Cost Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate costs that remain relatively constant in total within a defined relevant range and period.

### Illustrative Examples

- Monthly office rent
- Certain software subscriptions
- Fixed insurance premiums
- Contracted service fees

### Illustrative Scenario

A fictional business pays $2,000 in monthly rent.

| Monthly Orders | Total Rent | Rent per Order |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 | $2,000 | $20 |
| 200 | $2,000 | $10 |
| 400 | $2,000 | $5 |

All values are hypothetical.

### Interpretation

Total rent remains constant in this simplified example, while rent per order decreases as order volume increases.

### Important Consideration

Fixed costs may change when capacity requirements, contracts, or operating conditions change.

---

# 6. Variable Cost Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate costs that change with a defined level of business activity.

### Illustrative Examples

- Packaging materials per order
- Certain transaction processing fees
- Direct materials per unit
- Per-unit fulfillment charges

### Formula

**Total Variable Cost = Variable Cost per Unit × Activity Volume**

### Illustrative Example

A fictional business spends $3 on packaging for each order.

At 500 orders:

**Total Packaging Cost = $3 × 500 = $1,500**

### Potential Business Questions

- How do variable costs change with volume?
- Is variable cost per unit increasing?
- Are higher expenses explained by increased activity?
- Does cost behavior remain consistent across periods?

---

# 7. Mixed Cost Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate costs containing both fixed and variable components.

### Simplified Formula

**Total Mixed Cost = Fixed Component + (Variable Rate × Activity Volume)**

### Illustrative Example

A fictional business has a service expense consisting of:

- Fixed monthly fee: $100
- Variable charge: $0.50 per transaction
- Transactions: 400

**Total Cost = $100 + ($0.50 × 400) = $300**

### Important Consideration

The components of actual mixed costs may require estimation and validation.

Methods such as high-low analysis or regression may be explored in future projects.

---

# 8. Step Cost Analysis

Step costs remain stable over a defined activity interval and increase when activity crosses a capacity threshold.

### Illustrative Example

A hypothetical fulfillment service charges:

- $500 monthly for up to 200 orders.
- $900 monthly for 201–400 orders.

### Business Questions

- At what activity levels do costs increase?
- Are current resources near capacity?
- How do capacity thresholds affect cost per order?

### Important Consideration

Step costs should not be modeled as continuously variable when the contractual structure uses discrete tiers.

# 10. Expense Category Analysis

## Purpose

Understand how total spending is distributed across expense categories.

### Formula

**Expense Category Share % = Category Expense ÷ Total Defined Expenses × 100**

### Illustrative Example

| Expense Category | Hypothetical Amount | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Payroll | $12,000 | 60% |
| Rent | $3,000 | 15% |
| Marketing | $2,000 | 10% |
| Software | $1,000 | 5% |
| Other | $2,000 | 10% |
| **Total** | **$20,000** | **100%** |

### Interpretation

Payroll represents 60% of total defined expenses in this hypothetical example.

This does not establish that payroll spending is excessive.

### Potential Visualization

Sorted horizontal bar chart showing expenses by category.

---

# 11. Expense Trend Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate how expenses change across reporting periods.

### Potential Comparisons

- Month over month
- Quarter over quarter
- Year over year

### Formula

**Expense Growth % = (Current Period Expense − Previous Period Expense) ÷ Previous Period Expense × 100**

### Illustrative Example

- Previous month expenses: $10,000
- Current month expenses: $11,500

**Expense Growth = 15%**

### Interpretation

Expenses increased by 15% between the two periods.

Additional information is required to determine whether this change is favorable, unfavorable, or consistent with business activity.

# 12. Budget Versus Actual Expense Analysis

## Purpose

Compare planned spending with recorded expenses.

### Dollar Variance

**Expense Variance = Actual Expense − Budgeted Expense**

### Percentage Variance

**Expense Variance % = (Actual − Budget) ÷ Budget × 100**

### Illustrative Example

| Category | Budget | Actual | Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Marketing | $2,000 | $2,500 | +$500 |
| Software | $800 | $750 | −$50 |
| Utilities | $600 | $650 | +$50 |
| Supplies | $400 | $350 | −$50 |

### Interpretation

Marketing spending exceeded budget by $500.

This difference may warrant review, but it does not automatically indicate poor financial performance.

### Important Consideration

The analysis should distinguish numerical variance from business significance.

---

# 13. Favorable & Unfavorable Expense Variances

For a conventional expense budget:

- Actual spending below budget may be favorable.
- Actual spending above budget may be unfavorable.

However, the business context matters.

### Example

A marketing campaign exceeds its planned expense budget but generates additional profitable sales.

The spending increase should be evaluated alongside campaign results rather than in isolation.

### Reporting Standard

A variance report should include:

- Budget
- Actual
- Dollar variance
- Percentage variance
- Initial classification
- Explanation
- Follow-up question

# 14. Expense-to-Revenue Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate selected expenses relative to business revenue.

### Formula

**Expense-to-Revenue Ratio = Defined Expense ÷ Revenue × 100**

### Illustrative Example

- Marketing expense: $3,000
- Revenue: $30,000

**Marketing Expense Ratio = 10%**

### Potential Business Questions

- Is the expense ratio increasing?
- Are expenses growing faster than revenue?
- Does the ratio vary across periods?
- Are changes associated with business strategy?

### Important Consideration

An expense ratio alone does not establish return on investment or the effectiveness of spending.

---

# 15. Cost per Unit Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate cost relative to a defined unit of business activity.

### Formula

**Cost per Unit = Total Relevant Cost ÷ Number of Qualifying Units**

### Potential Measures

- Cost per order
- Cost per transaction
- Cost per shipment
- Cost per unit produced
- Cost per service request

### Illustrative Example

- Fulfillment cost: $4,000
- Orders fulfilled: 800

**Fulfillment Cost per Order = $5**

### Important Consideration

The cost numerator and activity denominator must cover comparable scopes and reporting periods.

# 25. Illustrative Excel Formulas

The following examples demonstrate syntax and should be adapted to the actual workbook.

### Total Expense by Category

```excel
=SUMIFS(Expenses[Amount],Expenses[Category],"Marketing")
```

### Expense by Vendor

```excel
=SUMIFS(Expenses[Amount],Expenses[Vendor],"Vendor A")
```

### Expense Variance

```excel
=ActualExpense-BudgetExpense
```

### Percentage Variance

```excel
=IFERROR((ActualExpense-BudgetExpense)/BudgetExpense,"N/A")
```

### Expense Share

```excel
=IFERROR(CategoryExpense/TotalExpense,"N/A")
```

### Cost per Order

```excel
=IFERROR(TotalFulfillmentCost/OrdersFulfilled,"N/A")
```

### Fixed Versus Variable Classification

```excel
=IF([@[Cost Behavior]]="Fixed","Fixed Cost","Other Cost Type")
```

### Important Consideration

Formula accuracy depends on the dataset structure, expense definitions, and selected reporting period.
