# Revenue and Profitability

## Purpose

This folder is for projects that examine how a business earns revenue and how much of it becomes profit.

> **Educational purpose.** Analyses in this section are learning exercises that use public or synthetic data. They are not professional accounting, tax, investment, or financial advice, and they should not be used to make financial decisions about a real business or person.

## Topics

| Topic | What to analyze |
|---|---|
| Revenue trends | Changes in revenue over time and by segment |
| Gross profit | Revenue minus cost of goods sold |
| Operating profit | Gross profit minus operating expenses |
| Net income | Profit after all expenses, interest, and taxes |
| Profit margins | Gross, operating, and net margins as percentages |
| Break-even analysis | Sales level at which revenue equals total cost |
| Profitability comparisons | Comparing products, segments, or periods |


## Calculation Reference

| Measure | Calculation method |
|---|---|
| Gross profit | Revenue minus cost of goods sold |
| Gross margin | Gross profit divided by revenue |
| Operating profit | Gross profit minus operating expenses |
| Net margin | Net income divided by revenue |
| Break-even units | Fixed costs divided by (price per unit minus variable cost per unit) |


## Interpretation Guidance

A higher revenue figure does not guarantee higher profit. Analyses should compare margins as well as totals and note factors such as seasonality, pricing changes, or one-time items.
