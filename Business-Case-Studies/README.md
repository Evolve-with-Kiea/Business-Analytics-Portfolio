# 1. Purpose of Business Case Studies

Business analytics involves more than collecting information, calculating metrics, or creating dashboards.

Effective analytical work begins with understanding what an organization needs to know and why that information matters.

A business case study provides an opportunity to demonstrate the complete analytical process, including:

1. Understanding a business challenge.
2. Defining the problem clearly.
3. Identifying business objectives.
4. Developing research questions.
5. Determining data requirements.
6. Obtaining appropriate information.
7. Preparing and validating data.
8. Selecting analytical methods.
9. Performing calculations and comparisons.
10. Interpreting findings.
11. Developing evidence-based recommendations.
12. Communicating conclusions and limitations.

The emphasis is on connecting analytical activities to real business questions rather than demonstrating software features without a defined purpose.

### Guiding Principle

**Start with the business question. Use data to investigate. Validate the evidence. Communicate what matters. Recommend what the findings reasonably support.**

# 2. Business Case Study Structure

This section is organized into three connected stages.

| Folder | Primary Focus | Main Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| [Business Problems](Business-Problems/README.md) | Problem definition, background, objectives, stakeholders, research questions, and scope | Business problem statement and analytical plan |
| [Data Analysis](Data-Analysis/README.md) | Dataset documentation, data preparation, analytical methods, validation, and findings | Reproducible analysis and evidence-based findings |
| [Recommendations](Recommendations/README.md) | Business implications, actionable recommendations, risks, limitations, and conclusions | Decision-oriented recommendations report |

Each stage serves a different purpose while contributing to the overall case study.

## Stage 1: Business Problems

The first stage establishes what needs to be investigated.

Key activities include:

- Identify the business issue.
- Describe the organization and operating environment.
- Explain why the problem matters.
- Identify relevant stakeholders.
- Establish business objectives.
- Develop research questions.
- Define relevant KPIs.
- Identify preliminary data requirements.
- Establish analytical scope.
- Define success criteria.

**Expected Outcome:** A clearly defined business problem and structured plan for investigation.

## Stage 2: Data Analysis

The second stage evaluates the available information and documents the analytical process.

Key activities include:

- Identify and describe the dataset.
- Confirm data permissions and source reliability.
- Evaluate completeness and quality.
- Clean and prepare data.
- Document transformations.
- Select appropriate analytical methods.
- Perform calculations and comparisons.
- Develop relevant metrics.
- Create analytical tables or visualizations.
- Validate results.
- Document findings and limitations.

**Expected Outcome:** Reliable, transparent, and reproducible analytical findings connected to the original research questions.

## Stage 3: Recommendations

The final stage explains what the findings may mean for the business.

Key activities include:

- Summarize validated findings.
- Identify business implications.
- Develop evidence-based recommendations.
- Consider alternatives.
- Evaluate expected benefits.
- Identify implementation risks.
- Assess feasibility.
- Prioritize potential actions.
- Propose performance measures.
- Document analytical limitations.
- Communicate conclusions.

**Expected Outcome:** A professional recommendations report explaining what decision-makers could reasonably consider based on the available evidence.

# 4. Analytical Problem-Solving Methodology

The following process will guide the development of future case studies.

### Phase 1 — Understand

Identify the business problem, stakeholders, objectives, and decision-making requirements.

### Phase 2 — Define

Develop research questions, establish scope, identify potential metrics, and determine success criteria.

### Phase 3 — Prepare

Obtain appropriate data, assess quality, document limitations, and perform necessary cleaning.

### Phase 4 — Analyze

Apply relevant analytical methods, calculations, comparisons, and visualizations.

### Phase 5 — Validate

Check calculations, reconcile totals where appropriate, review assumptions, and investigate unexpected results.

### Phase 6 — Interpret

Explain what the evidence indicates and distinguish observations from possible explanations.

### Phase 7 — Recommend

Develop practical actions supported by the findings, including expected benefits, risks, and alternatives.

### Phase 8 — Communicate

Present the analysis through clear written documentation, visualizations, dashboards, or executive summaries.

### Phase 9 — Reflect

Document lessons learned, technical challenges, limitations, and future improvement opportunities.

**Development Cycle: Learn → Understand → Practice → Apply → Validate → Document → Improve**

For additional guidance, see [Analytical Methodology](../Documentation/Analytical-Methodology.md).

# 12. How New Case Studies Will Be Added

Each project should follow a consistent development process.

1. **Select a Business Problem:** Choose a specific issue involving finance, accounting, sales, marketing, operations, or another relevant business function.

2. **Define the Scenario:** Identify whether the project is hypothetical, synthetic, public-data-based, or based on appropriately authorized information.

3. **Document the Business Problem:** Establish objectives, research questions, stakeholders, scope, and success criteria.

4. **Identify Appropriate Data:** Select a dataset that can reasonably support the questions being investigated.

5. **Follow the Analytical Methodology:** Prepare data, select methods, perform analysis, validate results, and document findings.

6. **Develop Recommendations:** Connect findings to business implications, potential actions, expected benefits, and limitations.

7. **Organize Project Evidence:** Include relevant workbooks, scripts, queries, visualizations, or reports where appropriate.

8. **Review Documentation:** Confirm that the project is understandable, reproducible where feasible, and professionally presented.

9. **Update Portfolio Progress:** Record the actual project status and completed milestones.

10. **Reflect and Improve:** Identify lessons learned and future technical or analytical development opportunities.

# 14. Data Security, Ethics & Analytical Integrity

Portfolio case studies should use data responsibly and accurately represent the work performed.

### Acceptable Data Sources

- Publicly available datasets with appropriate usage permissions
- Synthetic datasets
- Hypothetical business scenarios
- Authorized personal business data
- Appropriately anonymized information where disclosure is permitted

### Restricted Information

Do not publish:

- Confidential employer records
- Customer financial information
- Personally identifiable information
- Proprietary business reports
- Restricted transaction records
- Sensitive operational data

Anonymization does not automatically provide permission to publish confidential information.

### Analytical Integrity Standards

- Distinguish hypothetical scenarios from real business engagements.
- Document data sources and assumptions.
- Explain cleaning and transformation decisions.
- Validate calculations.
- Report findings that contradict expectations.
- Avoid unsupported causal claims.
- Disclose analytical limitations.
- Present projected benefits as estimates rather than guarantees.
- Do not claim completed projects or demonstrated skills without supporting evidence.
