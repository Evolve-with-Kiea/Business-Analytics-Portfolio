# Customer Analysis

## Customer Acquisition, Retention, Segmentation, Lifetime Value & Purchasing Behavior

### Marketing Analytics | Business Analytics Portfolio

## Project Overview

**Customer Analysis** is a dedicated section of my Business Analytics portfolio focused on understanding customer acquisition, purchasing behavior, repeat purchases, retention, and long-term customer value.

This folder explores how businesses can use customer-level and transaction-level data to identify purchasing patterns, evaluate customer relationships, and develop more informed marketing and business strategies.

While campaign performance analysis focuses on how marketing activities generate traffic and conversions, customer analysis examines what happens after customers are acquired, including whether they return, how frequently they purchase, and how their purchasing behavior changes over time.

My ongoing studies in Business Analytics and marketing, combined with my professional background in accounting, financial operations, and business systems, provide a foundation for developing customer-focused analytical skills.

Projects will emphasize responsible data handling, accurate metric definitions, reliable calculations, and evidence-based interpretation.

# 1. Purpose

Understanding customers requires more than knowing how many sales a business generates.

A business may attract new customers while experiencing low repeat purchasing.

Another business may have fewer new customers but generate a substantial portion of revenue from returning buyers.

Customer Analysis examines these differences by exploring how customers enter, interact with, and continue purchasing from a business.

### Central Business Question

**How can businesses use customer and transaction data to understand acquisition, retention, purchasing behavior, segmentation, and long-term customer value?**

### Potential Business Questions

- How many new customers are acquired?
- How many customers return?
- What percentage of customers make repeat purchases?
- How frequently do customers purchase?
- How much revenue comes from returning customers?
- Which customer segments have different purchasing patterns?
- How does retention vary across acquisition periods?
- What assumptions are needed to estimate customer lifetime value?

# 4. Understanding Customer Data

Customer analytics may use several types of data.

### Customer-Level Data

One record per customer.

Potential fields:

- Synthetic customer ID
- Acquisition date
- Acquisition channel
- Customer segment
- First purchase date
- Last purchase date

### Order-Level Data

One record per order.

Potential fields:

- Order ID
- Customer ID
- Order date
- Order value
- Sales channel
- Order status

### Order-Line Data

One record per product within an order.

Potential fields:

- Order ID
- Product ID
- Product category
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Line revenue

### Important Principle

**Customer counts, order counts, and order-line counts are different measures.**

A single customer may place multiple orders, and a single order may contain multiple products.

# 6. New Versus Returning Customers

## Purpose

Distinguish customers making their first qualifying purchase from customers who purchased previously.

### Definitions

**New Customer:** A customer making their first qualifying purchase during the reporting period.

**Returning Customer:** A customer making a qualifying purchase during the reporting period who has a qualifying purchase before that period.

### Potential Measures

- New customers
- Returning customers
- Revenue from new customers
- Revenue from returning customers
- Average order value by group

### Important Consideration

The dataset must contain sufficient historical information to identify first purchases accurately.

# 7. Repeat Purchase Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate how frequently customers make additional purchases.

### Repeat Purchase Rate

**Repeat Purchase Rate = Customers with Two or More Qualifying Purchases ÷ Customers in the Defined Population × 100**

### Hypothetical Example

- Customers in the analysis population: 200
- Customers with at least two purchases: 60

**Repeat Purchase Rate = 30%**

### Interpretation

Thirty percent of the defined customer population made at least two qualifying purchases during the selected observation window.

### Important Consideration

Repeat purchase rates depend on the observation period and customer eligibility.

---

# 8. Purchase Frequency

## Purpose

Evaluate the average number of orders placed by customers.

### Formula

**Purchase Frequency = Qualifying Orders ÷ Unique Purchasing Customers**

### Hypothetical Example

- Orders: 500
- Purchasing customers: 200

**Purchase Frequency = 2.5 Orders per Customer**

### Important Consideration

Purchase frequency should specify the reporting period and customer population.

---

# 9. Average Order Value

## Purpose

Evaluate the average value of qualifying orders.

### Formula

**AOV = Net Order Revenue ÷ Number of Qualifying Orders**

### Hypothetical Example

- Net order revenue: $25,000
- Qualifying orders: 500

**AOV = $50**

### Important Consideration

AOV should use order-level totals and a consistent treatment of discounts, refunds, taxes, and shipping.

---

# 10. Customer Retention Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate whether customers continue purchasing over time.

### Cohort-Based Retention

**Retention Rate = Customers from a Cohort Active in a Later Period ÷ Eligible Customers in the Original Cohort × 100**

### Hypothetical Example

- Original customer cohort: 100
- Customers purchasing during the selected later period: 25

**Retention Rate = 25%**

### Important Consideration

Retention definitions depend on the business model.

For a subscription business, retention may mean maintaining an active subscription.

For a retail business, retention may mean making another purchase within a specified period.

These measures should not be treated as interchangeable.

# 13. Customer Lifetime Value

## Purpose

Estimate the economic value associated with a customer over a defined relationship or forecast horizon.

### Simplified Historical Revenue Measure

**Historical Revenue per Customer = Total Qualifying Revenue ÷ Unique Customers**

This is a historical average, not a forecast of lifetime value.

### Illustrative CLV Model

A simplified model may estimate:

**CLV = Average Order Value × Purchase Frequency per Year × Expected Customer Lifespan in Years**

### Hypothetical Example

- AOV: $50
- Purchase frequency: 3 orders annually
- Expected lifespan: 4 years

**Estimated Revenue-Based CLV = $600**

### Important Consideration

This model is illustrative and does not account for gross margin, retention variation, discounting, or customer servicing costs.

A profitability-based CLV model requires additional assumptions and data.

---

# 14. Customer Acquisition Cost

## Purpose

Evaluate the cost of acquiring new customers.

### Formula

**CAC = Defined Sales and Marketing Acquisition Costs ÷ New Customers Acquired**

### Hypothetical Example

- Acquisition costs: $2,000
- New customers: 100

**CAC = $20**

### Important Consideration

The calculation must specify which costs are included.

Advertising-only cost per acquisition is not necessarily equivalent to fully loaded CAC.

# 17. RFM Analysis

RFM stands for:

- **Recency:** How recently a customer purchased.
- **Frequency:** How often a customer purchased.
- **Monetary Value:** How much the customer spent.

### Purpose

Explore differences in customer purchasing behavior.

### Potential Measures

| RFM Component | Example Measurement |
|---|---|
| Recency | Days since last qualifying purchase |
| Frequency | Number of qualifying orders |
| Monetary | Total net purchase value |

### Potential Segments

- Recent frequent purchasers
- Recent occasional purchasers
- Historically frequent but currently inactive purchasers
- Low-frequency purchasers

### Important Consideration

RFM scoring thresholds should be documented.

RFM does not establish customer motivation, satisfaction, or future behavior by itself.

# 24. Customer Data Privacy & Security

Customer analytics requires careful protection of personal information.

### Privacy Requirements

- Use synthetic customer identifiers.
- Do not publish customer names.
- Do not publish email addresses.
- Do not publish home addresses.
- Do not publish phone numbers.
- Do not publish payment information.
- Do not publish confidential customer records.
- Avoid releasing detailed records that could reidentify individuals.

### Important Consideration

Removing names and emails does not automatically make customer data anonymous.

Unique purchase patterns, dates, locations, and other details may still allow reidentification.

For this public portfolio, synthetic datasets are the preferred option.

# 26. Illustrative Excel Formulas

The following examples demonstrate syntax and should be adapted to the actual dataset.

### Days Since Last Purchase

```excel
=AnalysisDate-LastPurchaseDate
```

### Purchase Frequency

```excel
=IFERROR(TotalOrders/UniqueCustomers,"N/A")
```

### Average Order Value

```excel
=IFERROR(NetOrderRevenue/TotalOrders,"N/A")
```

### Repeat Purchase Rate

```excel
=IFERROR(RepeatCustomers/EligibleCustomers,"N/A")
```

### Cohort Retention Rate

```excel
=IFERROR(RetainedCohortCustomers/EligibleCohortCustomers,"N/A")
```

### Customer Acquisition Cost

```excel
=IFERROR(AcquisitionCosts/NewCustomers,"N/A")
```

### Simplified Revenue-Based CLV

```excel
=AverageOrderValue*AnnualPurchaseFrequency*ExpectedLifespanYears
```

### Customer Revenue Using an Order-Level Table

```excel
=SUMIFS(Orders[Net Revenue],Orders[Customer ID],A2)
```

### Important Consideration

Customer counts should use distinct customer identifiers where appropriate.

Aggregating transaction rows without considering dataset structure can overstate customers, orders, or revenue.
