# Marketing Analytics

## Campaign Performance, Customer Insights, Marketing ROI & Business Intelligence

### Business Analytics Portfolio | Marketing Performance & Decision Support

## Project Overview

**Marketing Analytics** is a dedicated section of my Business Analytics portfolio focused on using data to evaluate marketing performance, understand customer behavior, measure financial outcomes, and support evidence-based business decisions.

This section explores how organizations collect, organize, analyze, and interpret marketing data to better understand campaign effectiveness, customer acquisition, retention, purchasing behavior, and return on marketing expenditure.

My professional background in accounting and financial operations provides a foundation for evaluating financial performance, costs, reconciliations, and business reporting. Through my ongoing Business Analytics and marketing studies, I intend to expand that experience into marketing performance measurement, customer analytics, data visualization, and business intelligence.

The objective is to develop practical analytical skills that connect marketing activity with measurable business outcomes while recognizing the limitations of attribution, incomplete data, and observational comparisons.

Projects will use public or synthetic datasets and follow consistent standards for data preparation, KPI definitions, analytical methodology, validation, documentation, and responsible interpretation.

# 1. Purpose

Marketing generates information across campaigns, websites, customer transactions, advertising platforms, and digital channels.

However, marketing activity alone does not establish business effectiveness.

A campaign may generate substantial impressions without producing meaningful conversions.

A business may acquire new customers but struggle to retain them.

An advertising campaign may generate attributed revenue without covering the costs associated with those sales.

Marketing Analytics examines these relationships.

### Central Business Question

**How can businesses use marketing, customer, and financial data to evaluate performance, understand customer behavior, compare marketing investments, and identify opportunities for further investigation?**

### Supporting Business Questions

- Which campaigns are associated with meaningful business outcomes?
- How do campaign results vary across channels?
- Which metrics are appropriate for different marketing objectives?
- How do customers behave after acquisition?
- Are customers making repeat purchases?
- How does retention vary across customer groups?
- What revenue is attributed to marketing activities?
- How do marketing costs compare with attributed financial outcomes?
- What additional data or testing would improve decision-making?

# 3. How the Three Areas Connect

Marketing Analytics becomes more useful when campaign activity, customer behavior, and financial performance are examined together.

| Analytical Area | Primary Question | Business Perspective |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign Performance | How effectively are campaigns generating defined actions? | Marketing effectiveness |
| Customer Analysis | What happens after customers are acquired? | Customer behavior and retention |
| Marketing ROI | How do marketing costs relate to attributed financial outcomes? | Financial efficiency and contribution |

### Illustrative Business Scenario

A hypothetical business runs three digital advertising campaigns.

Campaign Performance analysis compares impressions, clicks, conversions, and acquisition costs.

Customer Analysis examines whether customers acquired through those campaigns return and make additional purchases.

Marketing ROI analysis compares advertising costs, attributed revenue, and contribution after relevant marketing expenses.

Together, these analyses provide a broader view of marketing performance.

**The goal is to understand not only whether marketing activities generate attention, but also how they relate to customer relationships and financial outcomes.**

# 8. Marketing KPI Framework

Different business questions require different measures.

| KPI Category | Example Metrics |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach, impressions, frequency |
| Traffic | Clicks, CTR, website sessions |
| Conversion | Conversions, conversion rate |
| Acquisition Efficiency | CPC, CPA, CAC |
| Customer Behavior | Repeat purchase rate, purchase frequency |
| Retention | Cohort retention rate |
| Customer Value | Average order value, estimated CLV |
| Marketing Financial Performance | ROAS, contribution after marketing |
| Budget Performance | Actual spend, budget variance |

### KPI Documentation Requirements

Each KPI should include:

- Name
- Business definition
- Formula
- Numerator and denominator
- Data source
- Reporting period
- Unit of measurement
- Attribution assumptions, if applicable
- Interpretation
- Limitations

### Important Principle

**Marketing KPIs should be selected according to the business objective rather than included simply because they are available.**

# 9. Marketing Funnel Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate how users move through defined stages of a marketing journey.

### Illustrative Funnel

**Impressions → Clicks → Website Visits → Conversions → Repeat Purchases**

### Potential Measures

- Click-through rate
- Click-to-visit rate
- Visit-to-conversion rate
- Repeat purchase rate

### Important Consideration

These stages may come from different systems with different counting methods.

Impressions, clicks, sessions, customers, and orders are not interchangeable.

Funnel calculations require consistent definitions and appropriate denominators.

# 16. Marketing Financial Performance

## Purpose

Connect marketing activities with relevant financial measures.

### Potential Metrics

- Marketing expenditure
- Advertising spend
- Attributed revenue
- ROAS
- Contribution before marketing
- Contribution after marketing
- Marketing budget variance

### Important Distinctions

**ROAS:** Attributed revenue divided by advertising spend.

**Revenue-Based Return:** Attributed revenue less defined marketing cost, relative to marketing cost.

**Profit-Based Marketing Return:** Attributed contribution after defined marketing costs, relative to marketing cost.

### Important Principle

**Marketing revenue and marketing profitability are different measures.**

# 18. Marketing Data Structure

Marketing datasets may contain information at different levels.

| Data Level | Example Records |
|---|---|
| Campaign | One row per campaign |
| Campaign-Day | One row per campaign per date |
| Channel | One row per marketing channel |
| Customer | One row per synthetic customer |
| Order | One row per transaction |
| Order Line | One row per product within an order |

### Important Principle

**The dataset's reporting grain must be defined before aggregation.**

Combining tables at incompatible levels can produce duplicate revenue, inflated conversion counts, or incorrect customer metrics.

# 25. Data Privacy & Ethical Reporting

Marketing and customer analytics may involve sensitive business or customer information.

### Privacy Requirements

- Do not publish customer names.
- Do not publish email addresses.
- Do not publish home addresses.
- Do not publish payment details.
- Do not publish confidential advertising account data.
- Do not publish restricted client results.
- Use synthetic customer identifiers.
- Use aggregate reporting where appropriate.
- Document dataset permissions.
- Avoid records that could reidentify individuals.

### Important Consideration

Removing direct identifiers does not automatically make customer data anonymous.

For public portfolio projects, synthetic data is generally the safest option.

# 26. Analytical Limitations

Potential limitations include:

- Synthetic data assumptions
- Incomplete campaign records
- Inconsistent attribution windows
- Platform reporting differences
- Missing customer interactions
- Short reporting periods
- Small sample sizes
- Incomplete customer histories
- Seasonal changes
- Unmeasured external influences
- Differences in marketing objectives
- Missing product or marketing costs

### Reporting Standard

Conclusions should reflect what the available data supports and clearly acknowledge material limitations.
