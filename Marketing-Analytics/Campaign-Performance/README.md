This folder explores how businesses use campaign data to evaluate audience exposure, engagement, website traffic, conversions, and acquisition efficiency.

# 1. Purpose

Marketing campaigns generate data across advertising platforms, websites, email systems, and other digital channels.

However, large numbers of impressions, clicks, or interactions do not automatically establish campaign success.

A campaign may generate substantial traffic without producing meaningful conversions.

Another campaign may reach fewer people but attract an audience more likely to complete a desired action.

Campaign Performance analysis examines these differences.

### Central Business Question

**How can businesses use marketing data to compare campaigns, evaluate conversion performance, identify acquisition costs, and determine which areas warrant further optimization?**

# 3. Marketing Campaign Objectives

Campaign objectives determine which performance metrics are most relevant.

| Campaign Objective | Potential KPIs |
|---|---|
| Brand Awareness | Reach, impressions, frequency |
| Website Traffic | Clicks, CTR, website sessions |
| Engagement | Interactions, engagement rate |

# 4. Core Campaign Performance Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | Number of times content was displayed | Platform-reported |
| Reach | Number of unique people or accounts reached under platform definitions | Platform-reported |
| Clicks | Recorded interactions with a designated link or advertisement | Platform-reported |
| Click-Through Rate | Clicks relative to impressions | Clicks ÷ Impressions |
| Conversions | Completed defined actions | Platform or analytics-reported |
| Conversion Rate | Conversions relative to a specified denominator | Conversions ÷ Eligible Visits or Clicks |
| Advertising Spend | Amount spent on a campaign | Reported expenditure |
| Cost per Click | Average spend per qualifying click | Spend ÷ Clicks |
| Cost per Acquisition | Average spend per qualifying acquisition | Spend ÷ Acquisitions |
| Cost per Thousand Impressions | Spend per 1,000 impressions | Spend ÷ Impressions × 1,000 |
| Frequency | Average impressions per reached person or account | Impressions ÷ Reach |

### Metric Documentation

Each metric should include its definition, source, reporting period, unit, and relevant limitations.

# 5. Impressions Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate how frequently campaign content is displayed.

### Potential Questions

- Which campaigns generate the most impressions?
- How do impressions change over time?
- Are impressions increasing with advertising spend?
- Does impression volume differ across channels?

### Important Consideration

Impressions represent content displays, not unique individuals.

A person may generate multiple impressions.

---

# 6. Reach Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate the estimated number of unique people or accounts exposed to campaign content.

### Potential Questions

- Which campaigns reach the largest audience?
- How does reach compare with impressions?
- Does audience exposure vary by campaign?

### Important Consideration

Reach definitions and measurement methods vary across platforms.

Reach figures from different platforms generally should not be added together as though they represent unique people across all channels.

# 7. Click-Through Rate

## Purpose

Measure the proportion of impressions associated with qualifying clicks.

### Formula

**CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100**

### Hypothetical Example

- Impressions: 20,000
- Clicks: 500

**CTR = 2.5%**

### Interpretation

The campaign generated 2.5 qualifying clicks per 100 impressions.

### Important Consideration

CTR does not establish whether the traffic resulted in valuable business outcomes.

---

# 8. Conversion Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate how frequently users complete a defined campaign action.

### Potential Conversion Actions

- Purchase
- Lead form submission
- Newsletter signup
- Resource download
- Appointment request
- Account registration

### Important Consideration

A conversion must be explicitly defined.

A newsletter signup and a completed purchase should not automatically be treated as equivalent outcomes.

---

# 9. Conversion Rate

## Purpose

Measure the proportion of eligible traffic that completes a defined conversion action.

### Formula

**Conversion Rate = Conversions ÷ Defined Denominator × 100**

### Hypothetical Example

- Landing page sessions: 1,000
- Completed signups: 50

**Session Conversion Rate = 5%**

### Important Consideration

Conversion rates may use clicks, sessions, users, or another denominator.

The denominator must be documented, and the numerator and denominator must use compatible attribution and measurement definitions.

# 11. Cost per Click

## Purpose

Measure average advertising spend per qualifying click.

### Formula

**CPC = Advertising Spend ÷ Clicks**

### Hypothetical Example

- Spend: $400
- Clicks: 200

**CPC = $2.00**

### Interpretation

The campaign incurred an average advertising cost of $2 per qualifying click.

### Important Consideration

Lower CPC does not necessarily indicate better overall campaign performance.

---

# 12. Cost per Acquisition

## Purpose

Measure advertising spend relative to a defined acquisition event.

### Formula

**CPA = Advertising Spend ÷ Acquisitions**

### Hypothetical Example

- Spend: $600
- Acquisitions: 30

**CPA = $20**

### Interpretation

The campaign incurred an average advertising cost of $20 per defined acquisition.

### Important Consideration

CPA should not be confused with fully loaded customer acquisition cost, which may include additional sales and marketing expenses.

---

# 13. Cost per Thousand Impressions

## Purpose

Evaluate the cost of advertising exposure.

### Formula

**CPM = Advertising Spend ÷ Impressions × 1,000**

### Potential Questions

- Which campaigns have lower exposure costs?
- Does CPM vary by audience?
- Does CPM change across reporting periods?

### Important Consideration

CPM measures exposure cost, not business value or conversion efficiency.

---

# 14. Campaign Funnel Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate the movement from initial campaign exposure to a defined conversion.

### Illustrative Funnel

**Impressions → Clicks → Landing Page Visits → Conversions**

### Potential Measures

- Click-through rate
- Click-to-visit rate
- Visit-to-conversion rate
- Overall conversion efficiency

### Hypothetical Example

| Funnel Stage | Count |
|---|---:|
| Impressions | 50,000 |
| Clicks | 1,000 |
| Landing Page Visits | 900 |
| Conversions | 45 |

### Illustrative Calculations

- CTR: 2%
- Click-to-visit rate: 90%
- Visit-to-conversion rate: 5%

### Important Consideration

Funnel stages must use consistent measurement definitions.

Differences between clicks and sessions may result from tracking, user behavior, attribution, or technical factors rather than a single identifiable cause.

# 15. Campaign Comparison Analysis

## Purpose

Compare campaigns using consistent definitions and appropriate context.

### Potential Comparison Dimensions

- Campaign objective
- Channel
- Audience
- Reporting period
- Spend
- Clicks
- Conversions
- CPC
- CPA
- Conversion rate

### Hypothetical Campaign Comparison

| Campaign | Spend | Clicks | Conversions | CPC | CPA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Campaign A | $500 | 250 | 20 | $2.00 | $25.00 |
| Campaign B | $600 | 200 | 30 | $3.00 | $20.00 |
| Campaign C | $400 | 200 | 10 | $2.00 | $40.00 |

### Illustrative Interpretation

Campaign B has the lowest cost per defined conversion, despite having the highest CPC.

This demonstrates why click cost and acquisition cost should be evaluated separately.

### Important Consideration

Campaigns should not be ranked without considering objectives, audience differences, attribution rules, conversion definitions, and data quality.

# 19. Campaign Budget Analysis

## Purpose

Compare planned campaign expenditure with actual spending.

### Formula

**Budget Variance = Actual Spend − Budgeted Spend**

### Percentage Variance

**Variance % = (Actual Spend − Budgeted Spend) ÷ Budgeted Spend × 100**

### Potential Questions

- Did the campaign remain within budget?
- Which campaigns exceeded planned spending?
- Were higher expenditures associated with additional conversions?

### Important Consideration

Budget compliance and campaign effectiveness are separate measures.

# 22. Campaign Optimization Framework

Campaign analysis may identify opportunities for further investigation.

### Potential Areas

- Low click-through rates
- High acquisition costs
- Landing page drop-offs
- Audience differences
- Creative performance changes
- Budget allocation

### Recommended Approach

1. Identify a performance question.
2. Review relevant metrics.
3. Check data quality.
4. Consider alternative explanations.
5. Form a testable hypothesis.
6. Recommend an appropriate experiment.
7. Define success criteria.
8. Evaluate results after testing.

### Important Principle

Recommendations should be framed as proposed tests or investigations when the available data does not establish causation.

# 26. Illustrative Excel Formulas

These examples demonstrate syntax and should be adapted to the actual workbook.

### Click-Through Rate

```excel
=IFERROR(Clicks/Impressions,"N/A")
```

### Conversion Rate per Visit

```excel
=IFERROR(Conversions/LandingPageVisits,"N/A")
```

### Cost per Click

```excel
=IFERROR(Spend/Clicks,"N/A")
```

### Cost per Acquisition

```excel
=IFERROR(Spend/Acquisitions,"N/A")
```

### Cost per Thousand Impressions

```excel
=IFERROR(Spend/Impressions*1000,"N/A")
```

### Campaign Spend by Channel

```excel
=SUMIFS(CampaignData[Spend],CampaignData[Channel],"Paid Social")
```

### Campaign Conversion Count

```excel
=SUMIFS(CampaignData[Conversions],CampaignData[Campaign Name],"Campaign A")
```

### Budget Variance

```excel
=ActualSpend-BudgetedSpend
```

### Important Consideration

For aggregate rates, calculate the ratio using the appropriate summed numerator and denominator rather than taking an unweighted average of individual campaign rates.

- Incomplete tracking
- Reporting delays

- Creative variations
