# Marketing ROI

## Marketing Return on Investment, Advertising Efficiency, Campaign Profitability & Financial Performance

### Marketing Analytics | Business Analytics Portfolio

## Project Overview

**Marketing ROI** is a dedicated section of my Business Analytics portfolio focused on evaluating the financial performance of marketing activities.

This folder explores how organizations measure marketing expenditure, analyze attributed revenue, calculate return on advertising spend, evaluate campaign contribution profit, and compare financial results across marketing channels.

While Campaign Performance analysis examines metrics such as impressions, clicks, conversion rates, and cost per acquisition, Marketing ROI focuses on the relationship between marketing investment and financial outcomes.

My professional background in accounting, financial operations, accounts receivable, accounts payable, bookkeeping, and reconciliations provides a foundation for understanding costs, revenue, financial accuracy, and business performance.

Through this portfolio, I intend to combine that experience with marketing analytics, financial modeling, Excel reporting, and business intelligence.

Projects will use public or synthetic datasets and emphasize consistent financial definitions, transparent attribution assumptions, reliable calculations, and responsible interpretation.

**Portfolio Category:** Marketing Analytics / Marketing ROI / Financial Performance Analysis

# 1. Purpose

Marketing activities require investments of money, time, and resources.

Businesses need to understand not only whether campaigns generate traffic and conversions, but also how those outcomes relate to financial performance.

A campaign may generate substantial attributed revenue while producing limited profit after product costs and marketing expenses.

Another campaign may generate less revenue but have stronger contribution margins.

Marketing ROI analysis investigates these relationships.

### Central Business Question

**How can businesses compare marketing expenditures with attributed revenue and contribution profit to evaluate financial performance and identify opportunities for further investigation?**

### Supporting Business Questions

- How much is being spent on marketing?
- Which campaigns are associated with more revenue?
- How does ROAS differ across channels?
- Are campaign costs covered by attributed contribution profit?
- How does profitability change when product costs are considered?
- Which financial assumptions influence marketing return calculations?
- How do attribution models affect reported results?
- What additional evidence would be needed to support budget decisions?

# 7. Return on Advertising Spend

## Purpose

Measure attributed revenue relative to advertising expenditure.

### Formula

**ROAS = Attributed Revenue ÷ Advertising Spend**

### Hypothetical Example

- Attributed revenue: $5,000
- Advertising spend: $1,000

**ROAS = 5.0**

### Interpretation

The campaign received $5 in attributed revenue for each $1 of advertising spend.

### Important Consideration

ROAS does not deduct product costs, fulfillment costs, or other operating expenses.

A high ROAS does not necessarily indicate profitability.

---

# 8. Revenue-Based Marketing Return

## Purpose

Evaluate attributed revenue less defined marketing costs, relative to those costs.

### Formula

**Revenue-Based Return = (Attributed Revenue − Marketing Cost) ÷ Marketing Cost × 100**

### Hypothetical Example

- Attributed revenue: $5,000
- Marketing cost: $1,000

**Revenue-Based Return = 400%**

### Important Consideration

This is a revenue-based measure, not a profit-based return on investment.

It does not account for product costs or other expenses.

The metric should be labeled clearly rather than presented as a comprehensive measure of marketing profitability.

---

# 9. Profit-Based Marketing Return

## Purpose

Evaluate marketing return after considering relevant product or service contribution costs.

### Simplified Formula

**Profit-Based Marketing Return = (Attributed Contribution Before Marketing − Marketing Cost) ÷ Marketing Cost × 100**

### Definitions

**Attributed Contribution Before Marketing:** Attributed net revenue less relevant variable costs associated with the attributed sales.

**Marketing Cost:** Defined campaign or marketing investment included in the calculation.

### Hypothetical Example

- Attributed net revenue: $5,000
- Associated variable costs: $2,000
- Attributed contribution before marketing: $3,000
- Marketing cost: $1,000

**Profit-Based Marketing Return = 200%**

### Interpretation

Under the hypothetical model, attributed contribution exceeds defined marketing costs by $2,000, equivalent to a 200% return relative to marketing costs.

### Important Consideration

This is a modeled, attribution-based return, not necessarily the incremental causal ROI of the campaign.

---

# 10. Campaign Contribution Profit

## Purpose

Evaluate the contribution remaining after relevant product costs and marketing expenses.

### Simplified Formula

**Campaign Contribution After Marketing = Attributed Net Revenue − Associated Variable Costs − Defined Marketing Costs**

### Hypothetical Example

| Financial Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Attributed Net Revenue | $5,000 |
| Variable Product Costs | $2,000 |
| Contribution Before Marketing | $3,000 |
| Marketing Costs | $1,000 |
| **Contribution After Marketing** | **$2,000** |

### Important Consideration

This is not necessarily net profit.

Additional fixed expenses, overhead, taxes, and other costs may remain.

# 11. ROAS Versus Marketing ROI

ROAS and ROI answer different questions.

| Measure | Main Question | Includes Product Costs? |
|---|---|---|
| ROAS | How much attributed revenue was generated per advertising dollar? | No |
| Revenue-Based Return | How much attributed revenue exceeded marketing spending? | No |
| Profit-Based Marketing Return | How much attributed contribution remained after defined marketing costs? | Yes, for included variable costs |
| Incremental Marketing ROI | What incremental profit was caused by marketing relative to its cost? | Requires an appropriate causal measurement approach |

### Important Principle

**ROAS measures attributed revenue efficiency, while profit-based return evaluates a more complete financial relationship. Neither automatically establishes causation.**

# 12. Contribution Margin Analysis

## Purpose

Understand how much attributed revenue remains after relevant variable costs.

### Formula

**Contribution Margin = Net Revenue − Variable Costs**

### Contribution Margin Ratio

**Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution Margin ÷ Net Revenue × 100**

### Potential Business Questions

- Do product costs affect marketing profitability?
- Which product categories have stronger contribution margins?
- Does the same ROAS produce different financial outcomes across products?

### Important Consideration

Contribution margin definitions must be consistent with the selected cost model.

---

# 13. Break-Even ROAS

## Purpose

Estimate the minimum ROAS needed to cover advertising expenditure under simplified assumptions.

### Formula

When the contribution margin ratio excludes advertising costs and all other relevant assumptions are held constant:

**Break-Even ROAS = 1 ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio**

### Hypothetical Example

- Contribution margin ratio: 40%

**Break-Even ROAS = 2.5**

### Interpretation

Under the simplified assumptions, $2.50 in attributed revenue is needed per $1 of advertising spend to cover the advertising cost.

### Important Consideration

This threshold does not necessarily cover additional marketing expenses, overhead, or taxes.

# 17. Marketing Budget Variance Analysis

## Purpose

Compare actual marketing expenditure with planned budgets.

### Formula

**Budget Variance = Actual Spend − Budgeted Spend**

### Percentage Variance

**Budget Variance % = (Actual Spend − Budgeted Spend) ÷ Budgeted Spend × 100**

### Potential Business Questions

- Which campaigns exceeded budget?
- Which channels spent less than planned?
- Did spending changes coincide with different financial outcomes?

### Important Consideration

A campaign that remains under budget is not necessarily more effective.

# 21. Attribution Models

Marketing attribution determines how conversion credit is assigned.

### Common Approaches

**Last-Click Attribution**

Credits the final qualifying marketing touchpoint.

**First-Click Attribution**

Credits the first qualifying marketing touchpoint.

**Linear Attribution**

Distributes credit across qualifying touchpoints according to a defined rule.

**Position-Based Attribution**

Assigns different weights to selected touchpoints.

**Data-Driven Attribution**

Uses modeled relationships to allocate credit, depending on the platform and methodology.

### Important Consideration

Different attribution models can produce different campaign return estimates.

# 29. Illustrative Excel Formulas

These examples demonstrate syntax and should be adapted to the actual workbook.

### Return on Advertising Spend

```excel
=IFERROR(AttributedRevenue/AdvertisingSpend,"N/A")
```

### Revenue-Based Return

```excel
=IFERROR((AttributedRevenue-MarketingCost)/MarketingCost,"N/A")
```

### Contribution Before Marketing

```excel
=AttributedNetRevenue-VariableCosts
```

### Contribution After Marketing

```excel
=ContributionBeforeMarketing-MarketingCost
```

### Profit-Based Marketing Return

```excel
=IFERROR(ContributionAfterMarketing/MarketingCost,"N/A")
```

### Break-Even ROAS

```excel
=IF(ContributionMarginRatio>0,1/ContributionMarginRatio,"N/A")
```

### Budget Variance

```excel
=ActualSpend-BudgetedSpend
```

### Campaign Marketing Spend

```excel
=SUMIFS(MarketingData[Marketing Cost],MarketingData[Campaign Name],"Campaign A")
```

### Important Consideration

Aggregate ROAS should generally be calculated from total attributed revenue divided by total advertising spend, not from an unweighted average of individual campaign ROAS values.
