# Excel Projects

## Business Analytics, Financial Reporting, Sales Dashboards & Performance Measurement

### Microsoft Excel | Business Analytics Portfolio

## Project Overview

**Excel Projects** is a dedicated section of my Business Analytics portfolio focused on developing practical spreadsheet skills through realistic business scenarios involving sales reporting, financial analysis, and business performance measurement.

The purpose of this section is to demonstrate how Microsoft Excel can be used to organize business data, perform calculations, identify trends, evaluate financial and operational performance, and communicate information through professional reports and dashboards.

My background in accounting and financial operations provides a foundation in financial reporting, reconciliations, transaction processing, and data accuracy. Through my Business Analytics studies, I am expanding those skills into data preparation, analytical modeling, KPI development, visualization, and business intelligence.

Rather than creating spreadsheets solely to demonstrate technical features, the objective is to build projects that address meaningful business questions.

Each project will follow a documented analytical process, use appropriate datasets, and explain the business significance of its findings.

**Portfolio Category:** Microsoft Excel / Business Analytics / Financial Analytics / Reporting

**Current Status:** Planned — Project Frameworks Established

**Primary Tool:** Microsoft Excel

**Potential Future Tools:** Power Query, Power BI, SQL, Python

**Data Approach:** Public, synthetic, or appropriately authorized and protected datasets

**Primary Learning Goal:** Develop a collection of documented Excel projects demonstrating analytical reasoning, technical skills, and business interpretation.

# 1. Purpose

This section is designed to develop practical Excel capabilities for analyzing business information and supporting decision-making.

Excel is widely used for financial reporting, operational analysis, business planning, and management reporting.

However, effective analysis requires more than familiarity with formulas and charts.

It also requires the ability to:

- Define a business problem.
- Identify relevant data.
- Select appropriate calculations.
- Validate information.
- Interpret results.
- Explain limitations.
- Communicate findings clearly.

### Central Business Question

**How can Microsoft Excel be used to transform business data into accurate calculations, meaningful performance insights, and decision-support reports?**

# 7. Integrated Business Analytics Framework

The three project areas represent complementary dimensions of business performance.

| Analytical Dimension | Primary Project | Example Business Question |
|---|---|---|
| Sales Performance | Sales Dashboard | What is driving sales activity? |
| Financial Performance | Financial Analysis | How profitable is the business? |
| Operational Performance | Business Performance | Is the business achieving its goals efficiently? |

### Integrated Example

A hypothetical business experiences a 15% increase in sales revenue.

A Sales Dashboard may identify which products contributed to the increase.

Financial Analysis may evaluate whether profit margins improved.

Business Performance may determine whether order processing and fulfillment remained efficient.

Together, these analyses provide a more complete view of business performance.

**The objective is to understand not only what changed, but also why the change matters.**

# 10. Data Validation & Quality Assurance

Each project should include appropriate validation procedures.

### Potential Validation Checks

- Reconcile transaction totals.
- Verify formulas.
- Review duplicate identifiers.
- Confirm reporting periods.
- Compare calculated totals with source data.
- Validate percentage calculations.
- Check for missing categories.
- Review assumptions.

### Financial Validation Examples

- Revenue totals reconcile.
- Expense categories sum correctly.
- Profit calculations follow documented definitions.
- Budget variance formulas are consistent.

### Operational Validation Examples

- KPI denominators are clearly defined.
- Actual and target periods are comparable.
- Status indicators follow documented rules.

# 13. Dashboard Development

Excel dashboards should provide a clear, organized summary of business performance.

### Recommended Dashboard Components

- Descriptive title
- Reporting period
- KPI summary
- Trend charts
- Category comparisons
- Actual-versus-target results
- Filters or slicers
- Explanatory notes

### Dashboard Design Principles

- Prioritize readability.
- Use consistent formatting.
- Avoid unnecessary visual clutter.
- Select charts based on analytical purpose.
- Label units clearly.
- Use appropriate number formats.
- Maintain consistent KPI definitions.
- Include data refresh information where relevant.

### Important Principle

**A dashboard should make business information easier to understand, not simply more visually attractive.**

# 14. Proposed Excel Project Workflow

Every project should follow a consistent process.

### Step 1: Define the Business Problem

Identify the question the analysis should answer.

### Step 2: Establish Objectives

Determine the expected analytical outcomes.

### Step 3: Select a Dataset

Use public, synthetic, or appropriately authorized and protected information.

### Step 4: Review the Data

Inspect fields, formats, missing values, and potential errors.

### Step 5: Clean and Prepare

Apply appropriate data preparation methods.

### Step 6: Define KPIs

Document formulas, reporting periods, and assumptions.

Use Excel formulas, PivotTables, and other appropriate techniques.

### Step 8: Validate Results

Check calculations and reconcile totals.

### Step 9: Analyze Trends

Identify relevant comparisons and patterns.

### Step 10: Build Visualizations

Create charts, scorecards, and dashboards.

### Step 11: Interpret Findings

Explain what the results support.

### Step 12: Develop Recommendations

### Step 13: Document the Project

Record methodology, findings, limitations, and lessons learned.
